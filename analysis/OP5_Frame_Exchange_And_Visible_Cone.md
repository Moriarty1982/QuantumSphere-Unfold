# Does frame exchange enforce a common cone?

2026-10-27. Restricted two-real-mode kinetic audit, continuing OP5_Common_Cone_Kinetic_Criterion.md.

Source anchor: 12_structural_core.tex states dual exchange of the first two sectors and M J=J M (label prop:tensorial-intertwining-m); it defines C=(I+J)/2. The source does not thereby provide explicit kinetic matrices A,B or prove their commutation with J. The following grants that stronger kinetic symmetry condition as an explicit assumption and tests its consequences. J=sigma_x is a chosen two-mode representation, not a native identification of all frames.

Let A=a I+b J and B=p I+q J be real symmetric matrices commuting with J. Assume a>|b|, p>|q| so the kinetic energy is positive and speeds real. The J-even/odd modes have

 c_+^2=(p+q)/(a+b), c_-^2=(p-q)/(a-b).

Their difference is 2(a q-b p)/(a^2-b^2). Hence exchange symmetry alone does not force a common cone; equality needs a q=b p. Counterexample A=I, B=I+(1/2)J is exchange symmetric and positive, yet c_+^2=3/2 and c_-^2=1/2. Both original labels are exchanged symmetrically; the propagation eigenmodes still differ.

Two distinct routes remain:

(1) Common kinetic cone: derive the extra relation a q=b p, equivalently B=c_*^2 A. As a sufficient auxiliary mechanism, an additional internal symmetry S=sigma_z with [A,S]=[B,S]=0 forces b=q=0 in this representation. Then both modes share speed sqrt(p/a). This extra symmetry has NOT been derived from the native model; identifying it with any frame or information operation is forbidden without a representation and action. It also does not fix p/a or an absolute vacuum coefficient.

(2) One operationally accessible sector: if the FULL dynamics (not only its principal part) preserve C=(I+J)/2 and the source/detector both couple only to its image, only c_+ is operational in that channel. This is selection of one cone, not equality of all cones. For constant C the decoupling requires every relevant generator term to preserve its image; a noncommuting interaction can restore access to the other mode. For variable C, derivatives of C add compatibility conditions. The scalar compensated projector C must not be silently identified with native P_C7, nor the selected even mode with all matter and photons.

The second route is particularly relevant to the earlier information-layer hypothesis: structural degrees of freedom could remain inaccessible to the detector without requiring equal speeds in every formal mode. But the actual native detector coupling and exact sector preservation must be shown. Local threshold closure does not establish this exact decoupling.

No choice between these routes is established here. The result is that J symmetry alone cannot close the causal compatibility gap. Native kinetic coefficients and operational projectors, not another arbitrary mixing parameter, are the discriminating data.
