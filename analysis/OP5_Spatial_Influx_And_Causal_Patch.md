# Spatial influx: a causal local current and its limits

2026-10-27. Necessary-current construction, not a spinor or common-action solution.

The homogeneous target of the earlier torsion witness requires cross current j_S0=2t. The restricted spin-balanced amplitude assignment had total density n=8t, violating conservation when all spatial currents vanish. A local influx can instead satisfy the density-form Ward identity on the same flat metric:

 J0=8t, Ji=-(8/3)xi, t>0.

Then partial_t J0=8 and sum_i partial_i Ji=-8, hence partial_mu J^mu=0 exactly. The Minkowski norm is -64t^2+(64/9)r^2. It is future timelike for r<3t, null at r=3t and spacelike outside. Thus this expression is a causal-current candidate only on a restricted patch; it cannot be used globally as a positive Dirac probability current.

For a fixed ball of radius R wholly within r<3t, Q_ball=8t*(4pi R^3/3), while the outward boundary flux is -(8/3)R*(4pi R^2). The continuity equation gives exactly the charge growth inside the ball from inward flux. This does not create charge. A finite reservoir cannot maintain this growth forever.

Crucial limitation: this is a current-level construction. The earlier upper-only resting spinors have identically zero spatial current and cannot realize it. The density n=8t and selected cross time component alone also do not prove that all spin, cross-spatial, coframe and probe equations can be realized simultaneously. One must introduce actual moving Dirac components and recompute those bilinears, rather than attach this current to the old amplitudes unchanged.

More generally for two classical positive-norm Dirac probes, |Re(A^dagger B)|<=(A^dagger A+B^dagger B)/2. If a fixed finite-volume patch has j_S0=2t everywhere, its total charge must be at least 4t*Vol(patch), even without the more restrictive spin-balanced factor of 8. A globally finite conserved total charge therefore cannot sustain that target on such a patch for arbitrarily long t. This bound assumes the same flat-volume/current setup and positive classical density; it is not a general statement for all curved or quantum models. Spatial transport repairs the LOCAL homogeneous continuity defect but does not remove the finite-reservoir restriction.
