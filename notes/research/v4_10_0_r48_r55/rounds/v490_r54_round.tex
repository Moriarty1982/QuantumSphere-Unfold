\paragraph{Purpose.}
Layer v4.9.0-r52 refuted the coupling forms K and S with one common \(\varepsilon\).  This layer records the pre-registered test H38 (note
\texttt{llm/Vorab\_Notiz\_H38\_Ein\_eps\_je\_Sektor.md}, commit \texttt{d3555bf}, fixed before computing) with one \(\varepsilon\) for each sector.
It also records a correction: v4.9.0-r52 (reading 3) states that one \(\varepsilon\) per sector gives as many free parameters as mixing quantities.
That count is wrong; each family has two \(\varepsilon\) for three angles, so one prediction per family remains.  Earlier layers are not rewritten.

\subsection*{v4.9.0-r54 results}
\label{subsec:v490-r54-results}

Forms K (\(DCD\)) and S (\(DCD^{-1}\)) of v4.9.0-r52 with independent \(\varepsilon_u,\varepsilon_d,\varepsilon_e,\varepsilon_\nu\) on the grid
\(0,0.01,\dots,6\); data as in v4.9.0-r50, neutrino masses restricted by \(\sum m_\nu<0.0642\) eV to \(m_1\in\{0,0.001,0.003\}\) eV.

\paragraph{Theoremlet v4.9.0-r54.1 (numerical).}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item[(a)] Form K: no quark combination gives \(|V_{us}|\), \(|V_{cb}|\), \(|V_{ub}|\) within \(\pm20\%\) (closest case \(20.2\%\) off); lepton combinations
reach the \(3\sigma\) ranges in \(0.003\%\) of the cases.
\item[(b)] Form S: quark combinations within \(\pm20\%\) in \(0.0009\%\) of the cases, in a connected region \(\varepsilon_u\in[1.36,1.63]\),
\(\varepsilon_d\in[1.42,1.47]\) (best case \(7\%\) off: \(0.2225\), \(0.0448\), \(0.0040\)); lepton combinations in the \(3\sigma\) ranges in \(0.009\%\),
in \(\varepsilon_e\in[0.44,0.82]\), \(\varepsilon_\nu\in[0.27,0.55]\), for all three admissible \(m_1\).
\item[(c)] In form S the remaining quantity is not sharp: among the combinations that fit \(|V_{us}|\) and \(|V_{cb}|\), \(|V_{ub}|\) ranges over
\([0.0034,0.029]\) with median \(0.018\), and \(0.9\%\) fit it; among those that fit \(\theta_{12}\) and \(\theta_{23}\), \(\sin^2\theta_{13}\) ranges over
\([0,0.99]\), and \(0.8\%\) fit it.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Proof sketch.}
Script \texttt{llm/k94\_test\_h38.py}, output \texttt{llm/k94\_out.txt}; (c) and the connectedness are checks after the evaluation, appended after the
first run (\texttt{k94\_out\_erstlauf.txt}), whose criterion values agree.  \(\square\)

\paragraph{Verdict and reading.}
H38a is refuted; H38b, c and d are confirmed.  By the criterion form S is a candidate for a reading with four \(\varepsilon\).
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item Formally confirmed, weak in content.  Form S with \(\varepsilon_u\approx1.4\)--\(1.6\), \(\varepsilon_d\approx1.45\), \(\varepsilon_e\approx0.4\)--\(0.8\),
\(\varepsilon_\nu\approx0.3\)--\(0.55\) fits both mixings with neutrino masses allowed by cosmology.  The one prediction left per family is not sharp: the
typical \(|V_{ub}|\) is five times too large, and the hits lie in the tail of a broad distribution.  This is a successful fit rather than an
explanation.
\item \(\varepsilon_u\) and \(\varepsilon_d\) are close, but no hit lies on \(\varepsilon_u=\varepsilon_d\) (v4.9.0-r52.1(b): closest case \(40\%\)); among
the leptons \(\varepsilon_e>\varepsilon_\nu\).
\item Open: why these values, and whether a coupling of \(C\) to the structure fixes the remaining quantity more sharply.
\end{enumerate}
