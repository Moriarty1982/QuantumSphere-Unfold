\subsection*{v4.10.0-r66 results}
\label{subsec:v4100-r66-results}

Notation and reading.  \(\beta\) stands for \(\beta_{\rm sym}=0.5493482\), \(\varepsilon=\operatorname{artanh}\beta=0.617447\) is the parameter of the
mask of H45, \(\gamma_L=(1+\beta^2)/(1-\beta^2)=1.86444\), and \(\alpha\) with \(\tan\alpha=\beta\) is the kick angle of r27.  On the Hopf sphere
\(S^2\) of \texttt{thm:bsym-hopf}, \(N\) is the photon pole, \(S\) its antipode and \(K_1\) the pure Hopf state at the polar angle
\(\theta_H=2\arcsin\beta=66.645^\circ\).  \textbf{Setting of this layer (reading C):} the Hopf sphere is read as the celestial sphere of light
directions, so that a point is a null direction, its spinor is the state, and a Lorentz boost acts on the sphere by aberration (as an element of
\(SL(2,\mathbb C)\) on the spinors).  The reading is set and not derived.

\paragraph{Theoremlet v4.10.0-r66.1 (two light directions, their overlap and the frame in which they are opposite; H161 K-e, not blind).}
Let two null directions enclose the angle \(\theta\), with spinors \(\psi_1,\psi_2\).  Then \(|\langle\psi_1|\psi_2\rangle|=\cos(\theta/2)\), and the
boost with velocity \(\beta=\cos(\theta/2)\) along their bisector makes them antipodal.  For equal energies \(E\) the invariant mass of the pair is
\(M=2E\sqrt{1-\beta^2}\).  Two frames moving with \(\pm\beta\) against the frame of the antipodes have the relative Lorentz factor
\((1+\beta^2)/(1-\beta^2)=\cosh2\varepsilon\).  \textbf{Confirmed.}
\emph{Proof sketch.}  Each direction encloses \(\theta/2\) with the bisector.  Aberration gives \(\cos\vartheta'=(\cos\vartheta-\beta)/(1-\beta\cos\vartheta)\),
which vanishes for \(\cos\vartheta=\beta\); both images are perpendicular to the boost axis on opposite sides.  The relative velocity of the two
frames is \(2\beta/(1+\beta^2)\).
\emph{Check.} k352 (Qiskit \texttt{quantum\_info}), k355 (sympy), k355b (Wolfram Language, exact, with the aberration formula instead of the
\(SL(2,\mathbb C)\) matrix).

\paragraph{Theoremlet v4.10.0-r66.2 (\(\beta_{\rm sym}\) is the overlap of the Hopf state with the antipode; H164 K-a, K-b, not blind).}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item[(a)] \(|\langle K_1|S\rangle|=\sin(\theta_H/2)=\beta\).  The angle between \(K_1\) and \(S\) is \(\pi-\theta_H=113.355^\circ\).  Among the 78
pairs of 13 named states of the record (listed in k355) the overlap equals \(\beta\) identically only for \((K_1,S)\) and its images under the exchange
and the tachyon map.  One further pair agrees only numerically: \((h,\text{exchanged }h)\) in HS(2) with \(2\beta^2/(1+\beta^4)=0.5532\) (\(+0.7\%\)), not an
identity.
\item[(b)] By r66.1 the boost that makes \(K_1\) and \(S\) antipodal has the velocity \(\beta_{\rm sym}\) and the rapidity \(\varepsilon\) of the mask
(H45).  The relative Lorentz factor of the frames \(\pm\beta\) is \(\gamma_L\), and \(\operatorname{sech}2\varepsilon=1/\gamma_L\) (v4.9.0-r24.3).
\end{enumerate}
H164 K-a and K-b \textbf{confirmed}.  In reading C the step in the proof of \texttt{thm:bsym-hopf} that equates the frame offset with
\(\sin(\theta_H/2)\) ``by the standard half-angle relation'' is the statement r66.1: the overlap of two light directions is the velocity of the frame
in which they are opposite.  The angle reading and the velocity reading of \(\beta_{\rm sym}\) are then one statement.

\paragraph{Theoremlet v4.10.0-r66.3 (the mixture of the two readouts is not the matter state; H164 K-c).}
The equal mixture of \(K_1\) and \(S\) has the Bloch length \(\beta=0.5493\) and the populations \((0.7747,\,0.2253)\).  The matter state of
Convention~F has the Bloch length \(1/\gamma_L=0.5364\) and the populations \((0.7682,\,0.2318)\).  The two agree only for
\(\beta^3+\beta^2+\beta-1=0\), \(\beta=0.5437\), a number without a meaning in the record.  H164 K-c \textbf{refuted}, as predicted.

\paragraph{Theoremlet v4.10.0-r66.4 (the boost carries the R-frame state to the populations of the matter state; H164 K-d).}
Under the boost of r66.2(b):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item[(a)] the angle between the images of \(N\) and \(K_1\) is \(2\alpha\) with
\[
\cos2\alpha=\frac{1-\beta^2}{1+\beta^2}=\frac1{\gamma_L},
\]
so \(\theta_H=66.64^\circ\) becomes \(57.56^\circ\);
\item[(b)] the populations of \(K_1'\) along the axis of \(N'\) are exactly \(1/(1+\beta^2)\) and \(\beta^2/(1+\beta^2)\), those of the matter state,
and \(K_1'\) dephased about this axis has the Bloch length \(1/\gamma_L\);
\item[(c)] at the Hopf value \(\beta^2=\tfrac13\): \(70.53^\circ\to60^\circ\) and \((\tfrac23,\tfrac13)\to(\tfrac34,\tfrac14)\), the pair of numbers in
the status note of \texttt{thm:bsym-hopf};
\item[(d)] the angle between \(N'\) and \(S'\) is \(\pi-2\alpha\), not \(\pi\); the images of the equator points lie at no named angle (for example
\(89.51^\circ\), \(50.77^\circ\)).
\end{enumerate}
\emph{Proof sketch.}  \((1-\cos\vartheta_{12})E_1E_2\) is invariant.  The Doppler factors of \(N\) and of \(K_1\) (and \(S\)) under the boost are
\(\gamma(1+\beta^2)\) and \(\gamma(1-\beta^2)\) with \(\gamma^2=1/(1-\beta^2)\).  With \(1-\cos\theta_H=2\beta^2\) this gives
\(1-\cos\vartheta'=2\beta^2/(1+\beta^2)\) for \((N,K_1)\) and \(2/(1+\beta^2)\) for \((N,S)\).
\emph{Verdict.}  The advance note asked whether the images lie ``on named angles''; its prediction was ``open (conjecture: refuted)''.  By the wording
K-d is \textbf{refuted} (item d).  The core, items (a)--(c), is \textbf{confirmed}.  Disclosed: the relation \(N'\)--\(K_1'=2\alpha\) was derived by hand
after the advance note was committed and before the first run.
\emph{Check.} sympy and Wolfram Language, all differences exactly zero.

\paragraph{Exploration v4.10.0-r66.5 (the Hopf value and four equivalent readouts; no criterion).}
At \(\beta=1/\sqrt3\) the angle between the two readouts is \(\arccos(-\tfrac13)=109.47^\circ\), the tetrahedral angle.  Four states with pairwise
overlap \(1/\sqrt3\) form a tetrahedron, and their projectors sum to twice the identity (the symmetric informationally complete measurement of a
qubit).  Four pairwise equiangular directions exist only at this angle; at \(\beta_{\rm sym}\) (\(113.36^\circ\)) at most three.  In reading C the Hopf
gap of \texttt{rem:bsym-hopf-gap} is the distance from an arrangement that closes to four equivalent readouts.  Whether this is connected with the
\(\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z\) orbit of that remark (three kernels at equal distance from the pole) is not investigated.
