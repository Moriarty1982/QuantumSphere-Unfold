\subsection*{v4.10.0-r53 setting}
\label{subsec:v4100-r53-setting}

A counting rule of a frame is an equation \(\sum_j g(j)\,e^{-2\pi\gamma a(j)}=1\) with \(a(j)=\sqrt{j(j+1)}\), \(j\in\{\tfrac12,1,\tfrac32,\dots\}\); the weight is
\(g(j)=2\) for M (Domagala--Lewandowski), \(g(\tfrac12)=2\) and \(g(j)=0\) otherwise for T (ABCK), and \(g(j)=2j+1\) for the full SU(2) count.  With
\(r_j=e^{-2\pi a(j)}\) it reads \(\sum_j g(j)\,r_j^{\gamma}=1\), the similarity (Moran) equation of a family of \(g(j)\) contractions with ratio \(r_j\) for each \(j\).

Result 148, as stated in its full text: for every finite indexed family \(\varphi_i(x)=r_ix+t_i\) on \(\mathbb R\) with \(0<|r_i|<1\) and every strictly
positive probability vector \(p\), the self-similar measure satisfies \(\dim_H\mu=\min\{1,h_{\rm RW}/\chi\}\), where \(h_{\rm RW}=\lim_n H(G_n)/n\) is the entropy
rate of the random compositions \(G_n\), counted by distinct affine maps, and \(\chi=-\sum_ip_i\log|r_i|\).  An exact overlap is an equality of complete
affine maps for distinct words of equal length; such overlaps are allowed.

Result 149, as stated in its full text: for every finite weakly reversible reaction network (each reaction \(y\to y'\) has a directed path back) with
positive constant rates and mass-action kinetics \(\dot x=\sum\kappa_{y\to y'}x^y(y'-y)\), every strictly positive solution exists for all \(t\ge0\), and there
is \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\) with \(\varepsilon\le x_i(t)\le\varepsilon^{-1}\) for all \(i\) and \(t\ge0\).

C5 as recorded: the selective collar recursion gives \(C5_M^{(n)}=(3/4)^n\) and \(C5_T^{(n)}=1-(3/4)^n\) (r20.4(b)); no purely multiplicative recursion
with \(q>0\) reaches zero at a finite stage (v4.8.0-r86, r94); form (iii) asks for a witness outside the iteration, and the null-instant candidate
fails because the score is \(1/2\) where the floor vanishes (r21.12).

\subsection*{v4.10.0-r53 results}
\label{subsec:v4100-r53-results}

\paragraph{Theoremlet v4.10.0-r53.1 (the counting equation is a dimension formula).}
(a) With the Gibbs weights \(p_{j,\cdot}=r_j^{\gamma}\) of a counting rule, \(H(p)/\chi=\gamma\) exactly.  (b) For every finite truncation of a counting
rule realised by distinct maps without exact overlaps, result 148 gives \(\dim_H\mu=\gamma\), since \(\gamma<1\).  (c) In units of the T quantum,
\(D=\gamma/\gamma_T\) exceeds one for M (\(1.8647\)), and result 148 says nothing there.
\emph{Proof.} (a) \(-\log p_{j,\cdot}=2\pi\gamma a(j)\) and \(-\log r_j=2\pi a(j)\), hence \(H(p)=\sum p\,2\pi\gamma a=\gamma\chi\).  (b) Without exact overlaps
\(H(G_n)=nH(p)\), and \(\min\{1,\gamma\}=\gamma\).  (c) The formula saturates at one on the line.  Numerically \(\gamma_M=0.237533\), \(\gamma_T=0.127384\),
\(\gamma_P=0.274067\), and \(H(p)/\chi=\gamma_M\) to fifteen digits; truncation at \(j\le200\) changes the equation by about \(10^{-130}\).  \(\square\)

\paragraph{Theoremlet v4.10.0-r53.2 (weight two needs two distinct maps).}
If the maps of \(|j,+j\rangle\) and \(|j,-j\rangle\) coincide for every \(j\), or only one of them is admitted, the rule of M becomes \(\sum_jr_j^{\gamma}=1\) with
\(\gamma_1=0.161586\) (\(=1.2685\,\gamma_T\)), and the rule of T leaves one map, whose attractor is a point (dimension 0).  In a truncated system
(\(j\le1\)) with generic translations the entropy rate stays at \(\gamma=0.2018\) for distinct maps and for reflected copies with their own centre,
drops to \(0.0966\) at the same Gibbs weights for identical copies, and falls with the word length (\(0.2018\), \(0.1725\), \(0.1628\), \(0.1579\) for
\(n=1,\dots,4\)) for copies reflected about the same centre.
\emph{Proof.} Coinciding maps merge their weights in the law of \(G_n\); the maximum of \(h_{\rm RW}/\chi\) over \(p\) is then the root of \(\sum_jr_j^{\gamma}=1\).
Admitting one state gives the same equation.  The rates are computed by counting distinct composed maps (Wolfram, script in the notes).  \(\square\)

\paragraph{Theoremlet v4.10.0-r53.3 (order of punctures).}
Two contractions \(\varphi_a,\varphi_b\) commute exactly when they have the same fixed point.  If all compositions of equal multiset agree, all maps
share one fixed point and the attractor is that point.
\emph{Proof.} \(\varphi_a\varphi_b(x)-\varphi_b\varphi_a(x)=t_a(1-r_b)-t_b(1-r_a)\), which vanishes iff \(t_a/(1-r_a)=t_b/(1-r_b)\).  A common fixed point of all maps
is fixed by the whole system, so the attractor is that point.  \(\square\)

\paragraph{Remark v4.10.0-r53.4 (reading: orientation and U2).}
\(m\mapsto-m\) reverses the orientation of a puncture.  By r53.2 the weight two of M requires a readout that distinguishes the two orientations
without selecting one; identifying or selecting gives \(\gamma_1\).  This fits r31.1 (H118: the record does not fix the orientation) and states U2 as a
question about the readout (cross-section Q1 of the open problems).  By r53.3 a count of punctures without order cannot be represented by exact
overlaps without collapsing the attractor.  The reading neither derives U2 nor the rule \(j=\tfrac12\) of T; \(\gamma_1\) is no target value.

\paragraph{Theoremlet v4.10.0-r53.5 (frame exchange with a return path keeps \(C5_M\) positive).}
Model the exchange between the matter and the tachyon frame as a finite mass-action network with positive constant rates.  If the network is weakly
reversible, then by result 149 there is \(\varepsilon>0\) with \(C5_M(t)\ge\varepsilon\) for all \(t\ge0\).  Examples: \(M\rightleftharpoons T\) gives
\(C5_M(t)=\tfrac{k_2}{k_1+k_2}+\bigl(x_0-\tfrac{k_2}{k_1+k_2}\bigr)e^{-(k_1+k_2)t}\ge\min\{x_0,\tfrac{k_2}{k_1+k_2}\}\); \(M+T\rightleftharpoons2T\) has the positive equilibrium
\(k_2/(k_1+k_2)\); a weakly reversible network of three frames starting at \(C5_M=10^{-5}\) never falls below its start (numerical).  The one-way exchange
\(M\to T\), the continuous counterpart of the collar recursion, is not weakly reversible: \(C5_M=x_0e^{-kt}\) is positive for every finite \(t\) and tends to
zero.  Hence \(C5_M\to0\) requires an exchange without any return path.
\emph{Proof.} Result 149 for the first statement; the examples are solved exactly and checked by substitution (residual 0), the three-frame case by
numerical integration (Wolfram, script in the notes).  \(\square\)

\paragraph{Theoremlet v4.10.0-r53.6 (no finite zero for mass action; classical).}
No mass-action network, weakly reversible or not, takes a positive concentration to zero in finite time.  A finite zero of a quantity that is
continuous in time requires a vector field that is not Lipschitz at zero (e.g.\ \(\dot x=-c\sqrt x\), zero at \(t^*=2\sqrt{x_0}/c\)) or a sign change of a signed
or complex quantity.  For a score \(S=N^2\) a transversal zero of \(N\) is a double zero of \(S\).
\emph{Proof.} Every term that consumes species \(i\) contains \(x_i\) as a factor, so on \([0,t]\) one has \(\dot x_i\ge-K x_i\) with \(K\) bounded along the solution, and
\(x_i(t)\ge x_i(0)e^{-Kt}>0\).  If the field is Lipschitz at zero and zero is an equilibrium, uniqueness forbids reaching it in finite time.  The example
and \(S'=2NN'\) are checked directly.  \(\square\)

\paragraph{Remark v4.10.0-r53.7 (consequence for form (iii) of C5).}
A witness outside the iteration cannot be a mass-action dynamics (r53.6), and if it is to approach zero at all it must be one-way (r53.5).  Two
classes remain: a dynamics that is not Lipschitz at the zero, of which the record contains no example (not searched systematically), and a sign change,
which for the original score failed (r21.12).  Modelling C5 as a reaction network is a setting; the record defines C5 by a discrete recursion and does
not state whether the exchange between the frames has a return path (the frame maps are invertible, which does not make a dynamics reversible).
For a weakly reversible exchange network the lower bound \(\varepsilon\) is a named persistent obstruction in the sense of the return contract of C6/T2,
for this model class only.
