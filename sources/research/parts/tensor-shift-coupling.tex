\section{Tensor Activation and Covariant Coupling Readout}
\label{v480:tensor-shift-coupling}

The preceding numerical test used identity input and output metrics.
Its ratio obstruction must not be extended without qualification to
an independently supplied, nontrivial tensor comparison. Conversely,
activating tensor notation does not by itself supply such a comparison.
The governing shifted normal form remains that of
Definition~\ref{def:v400-r05-governing-shifted-normal-form-object}.

\subsection{Which Shift Can Affect a Numerical Spectrum?}

The global QAM migration $\Sigma_4(k)=k+1$ moves namespace tags.
Definition~\ref{def:v400-r01-shifted-tensor-payload-migration}
preserves tensor sidedness, variance and contraction discipline;
Theorem~\ref{thm:v400-r01-shifted-activation-conservativity}
preserves the numerical readout payload and its guards. For example,
support $\{0,2,5\}$ becomes $\{1,3,6\}$, so its packed mask
changes from $37$ to $74$, not $38$. This is not an addition of one
to a coupling coefficient, phase or eigenvalue. The local first-boundary
HC shift of Definition~\ref{def:v3360-r16-first-boundary-shift}
has a different structural role; it is not a numerical mass law either.

The active two-sided grammar does require explicit comparison objects
for same-variance pairings
(Definition~\ref{def:two-sided-admissible-contractions}).
This exposes a genuine possible extension of the calculation: specify
the comparison on the transition output and the kinetic normalization
on the generation input. The residual-channel comparison in the source
is not automatically either of these metrics. Equal dimensions do not
identify their index spaces or physical roles.

\subsection{A Positive-Metric Tensor Extension}

Let $B:V_{\rm gen}\to W_{\rm out}$ be a finite complex map, with
specified positive Hermitian metrics $K$ and $H$ on these two spaces.
Here $H$ is dimensionless comparison data, not the energy-valued
complementary block $H_\perp$; $K$ is an additional kinetic metric,
not an identification with a spacetime Lorentz metric. In components,
\[
 (Q_H)_{ij}=\overline{B^a{}_i}\,H_{ab}B^b{}_j,\qquad
 Q_H=B^*HB,\qquad B^\sharp=K^{-1}B^*H.
\]
Both metrics and any change in $B$ must be specified independently of
the desired masses. This is an added positive finite realization of
the tensor grammar, not a native metric extraction.

\begin{proposition}[Metric-covariant effective spectrum]
\label{v480:tensor-generalized-spectrum}
The generalized eigenvalues of $Q_Hv=qKv$ are the nonnegative
eigenvalues of
\[
 C=H^{1/2}BK^{-1/2},\qquad
 Q_{K,H}=C^*C=K^{-1/2}B^*HBK^{-1/2}.
\]
In the additional weighted self-adjoint flat-gap model
\[
 \Omega_{K,H}=
 \begin{pmatrix}0&\mu B^\sharp\\ \mu B&\Delta I\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle=\operatorname{diag}(K,H),
\]
the corresponding light magnitudes obey
$m_i(m_i+\Delta)=\mu^2q_i$ for $\mu,\Delta>0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Set $w=K^{1/2}v$ in the generalized equation. Conjugating the block
operator by $\operatorname{diag}(K^{1/2},H^{1/2})$ gives the ordinary
Hermitian block $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&\mu C^*\\
\mu C&\Delta I\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. A singular-value
decomposition of $C$ splits its nonzero modes into $2\times2$ blocks,
whose characteristic equations give the stated identity.
\end{proof}

For $K=H=I$ this recovers the previous test exactly. The effective
quadratic mass readout and the flat-gap realization remain additional
hypotheses. A common phase of $B$ cancels even for nontrivial $K,H$.

\begin{proposition}[A covariant chart shift is not a physical correction]
\label{v480:tensor-chart-covariance}
For invertible input and output chart matrices $A,D$, with coordinates
$x=Ax'$ and $y=Dy'$, put
\[
 B'=D^{-1}BA,\quad K'=A^*KA,\quad H'=D^*HD.
\]
Then $Q'_H=A^*Q_HA$, and the generalized eigenvalues are unchanged.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Substitute the transformed tensors to obtain the congruence identity.
The substitution $v=Av'$ bijects the generalized eigenvectors and
preserves eigenvalue multiplicities; equivalently the characteristic
determinant is multiplied by $|\det A|^2$.
\end{proof}

The numerical audit also tests nonunitary charts. Correctly transporting
both metrics recovers the original eigenvalues within
$2\times10^{-15}$ absolute numerical error. Incorrectly resetting both
metrics to the identity after that change gives ratios approximately
$(0.0001813510,0.0416327127,1)$ instead of
$(0.0002509434,0.0602153365,1)$. Such a change is a contraction error,
not evidence of a physical frame effect. The chosen charts illustrate
covariance; they are not the native frame transformations.

\subsection{A Physical Comparison Can Change the Shape, But Must Be Derived}

\begin{proposition}[Positive comparison fit freedom]
\label{v480:tensor-comparison-fit-freedom}
For square invertible $B$, $K=I$ and any positive Hermitian target
$T$, the choice $H=(B^{-1})^*TB^{-1}$ is positive and gives
$B^*HB=T$. In particular, for
$B=F\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{q_i})F^*$ with $q_i>0$, setting
$H=F\operatorname{diag}(t_i/q_i)F^*$ realizes any $t_i>0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Positivity follows by applying $T$ to $B^{-1}w\ne0$; multiplication
gives the first identity. The aligned diagonal formula is its special
case.
\end{proof}

For the historical benchmark only, choose
$t_i=q_\tau m_i/m_\tau$ so the largest-mode weight stays one.
The required output eigenweights in the ascending $B$ modes are
\[
 (h_e,h_\mu,h_\tau)
 \simeq(1.14601907,\ 0.98751126,\ 1).
\]
Thus relative comparison weights of about $+14.60\%$ and $-1.249\%$
can absorb the original leading-ratio discrepancy. This is a
\emph{post-hoc metric fit}, not a prediction: the benchmark defines
$H$, including its eigenbasis alignment. It neither proves a tensor
activation law nor uniquely attributes the discrepancy to the phase.
The finite-gap correction still applies. An exact prescribed-mass
fit at given $\mu,\Delta$ would instead require
$t_i=m_i(m_i+\Delta)/\mu^2$, again target-derived.

\begin{proposition}[Absolute metric weights bound any scale enhancement]
\label{v480:tensor-metric-scale-bound}
If $h_-I\leq H\leq h_+I$ and $k_-I\leq K\leq k_+I$, with
positive bounds, the ascending eigenvalues satisfy
\[
 \frac{h_-}{k_+}\lambda_i(B^*B)
 \leq q_i(K,H)\leq
 \frac{h_+}{k_-}\lambda_i(B^*B).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Bound the generalized Rayleigh quotient
$x^*B^*HBx/(x^*Kx)$ above and below by the corresponding ordinary
quotient of $B^*B$, then apply the min--max principle on subspaces.
\end{proof}

Keeping the numerical $B$, $\mu=\mu_{\rm EW}$, $A=0$ and the
canonical flat gap $\Delta=m_{\rm P}$, the tau mass would require
\[
 \frac{h_+}{k_-}\geq
 \frac{m_\tau(m_\tau+m_{\rm P})}
      {\mu_{\rm EW}^2q_\tau}
 \simeq7.59\times10^{14}.
\]
The modest fitted shape weights above cannot supply that enhancement.
This bound concerns absolute relative normalization, not merely metric
condition numbers: even $H=cI$ has condition number one. Taking such
a huge scalar at fixed numerical $B,\mu$ enhances the canonical
coupling norm by about $2.755\times10^7$; it introduces the missing
physical coupling scale rather than deriving it. If it is only a
coordinate change, covariance cancels the apparent enhancement.
The physical normalization convention is now $\operatorname{Tr}(C^*C)$,
not an untransported coordinate trace $\operatorname{Tr}(B^*B)$.

\subsection{Hilbert-Carrier Enlargement Also Needs an Interaction}

\begin{proposition}[An identity tensor extension preserves the readout]
\label{v480:tensor-identity-extension}
For a normalized ancilla $a$, the isometry $Jv=v\otimes a$ and
$B'=B\otimes I$ satisfy
\[
 J^*B'^*B'J=B^*B.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The left side equals $(B^*B)\langle a,a\rangle=B^*B$.
\end{proof}

This rules out a correction from mere duplicated channel counting,
not from genuine new cross-channel interactions. The inherited
HS(5)/HS(6) carrier and activation-mask grammar does not itself
evaluate those interactions. A physical activation must supply the
new matrix entries, comparison tensors, seed embedding and normalized
gap/direct blocks together. The current source and role checker give
admissibility rules, not numerical $K,H$ on these particular spaces.
Consequently the old identity-metric exclusion is conditional, while
neither the namespace shift nor tensor activation alone establishes
the missing spectrum or scale.
