\section{Checks at compile time}\label{sec:tex}

A proof assistant checks rules about an abstract record.  The record that people actually read is a \LaTeX{} document.  The package
\texttt{qam.sty}~\cite{repo} brings the rules of \texttt{QSFormal.Provenance} into that document: claims are declared where they are made,
and the compiler stops with an error as soon as a declaration breaks a rule.

{\sloppy\paragraph{Commands.}  \verb|\qamframe{M}| declares a frame and \verb|\qammap{G}{M}{T}| a comparison map from \(M\) to \(T\).
\verb|\qaminput{k}{M}{s}| declares a value put in by hand, with frame \(M\) and status \(s\); its only input is itself.
\verb|\qamclaim{k}{M}{s}| declares a premise without inputs, for instance a theorem.  \verb|\qamderive{k}{s}{p1,p2,...}| derives a claim
from premises, \verb|\qamconvert{k}{s}{G}{p}| moves a premise into another frame, \verb|\qamcompare{a}{b}| records a comparison, and
\verb|\qamindependent{k}| asserts that a claim rests on no input.  \verb|\qamshow{k}| prints status, frame and inputs.\par}

\paragraph{Checks.}  \verb|\qamderive| and \verb|\qamconvert| stop when the declared status is higher than that of the weakest premise
(promotion); \verb|\qamderive| and \verb|\qamcompare| stop when premises of different frames meet, and \verb|\qamconvert| when the map does
not start in the premise's frame (frame mixing); \verb|\qamindependent| stops when the claim rests on an input (input read as result).  The
package also refuses a key that is used before it is declared or declared twice, so a document must be written in the append-only order
of Section~\ref{sec:case}; because of that order a single compiler pass is enough.  An option \verb|mode=warning| turns the errors into
warnings.

\paragraph{Example.}  The frame offset of the case study is computed from two literature values.  Declared honestly,
\begin{lstlisting}
\qamframe{M} \qamframe{T} \qammap[G]{G}{M}{T}
\qaminput[\gamma]{gamma}{M}{computed}          % two literature values
\qamclaim[\kappa]{counting}{M}{proved}         % the counting formula
\qamderive[\beta]{beta}{computed}{gamma,counting}
\qamconvert[\beta']{betaT}{conditional}{G}{beta}
\end{lstlisting}
\qamframe{M}\qamframe{T}\qammap[G]{G}{M}{T}%
\qaminput[\gamma]{gamma}{M}{computed}\qamclaim[\kappa]{counting}{M}{proved}%
\qamderive[\beta]{beta}{computed}{gamma,counting}\qamconvert[\beta']{betaT}{conditional}{G}{beta}%
it compiles, and \verb|\qamshow{beta}| prints \qamshow{beta}; the conversion gives \qamshow{betaT}.  Both are typeset by the package
while this paper is compiled.  The two errors of the case study now stop the compiler:
\begin{lstlisting}
\qamderive{beta}{proved}{gamma,counting}
! Package qam Error: Promotion: `beta' is declared `proved', but its weakest
(qam)                premise `gamma' is only `computed'.

\qamindependent{beta}
! Package qam Error: `beta' is cited as independent of inputs, but it rests
(qam)                on: gamma.
\end{lstlisting}
A comparison of \texttt{betaT} with a claim of frame \(M\) stops with ``Frame mixing: \dots\ are compared without a declared map''.

\paragraph{Generated notation.}  The declarations contain everything the QAM notation of Section~\ref{sec:language} needs, so the package
writes it.  The optional first argument of a declaration is the symbol; \verb|\qam{k}| typesets the claim in QAM notation with its status,
\verb|\qam*{k}| without, and \verb|\qamledger| a table of all claims in the order of declaration.  For the example, \verb|\qam{gamma}|,
\verb|\qam{beta}| and \verb|\qam{betaT}| give
\begin{gather*}
\qam{gamma}\\
\qam{beta}\\
\qam{betaT}
\end{gather*}
--- the input as the readout of a witness \(w_\gamma\), the derived value with its premises and the witness set \(W\) it rests on, and the
conversion with source and target frame of the map.  Nothing here is typed by hand, so the notation cannot drift from the declarations.
With the option \verb|export| every claim is also written, one line per claim, to a file \verb|\jobname.qam|, for instance
\begin{lstlisting}
beta := derive(gamma,counting); frame M; status computed; inputs {gamma}
\end{lstlisting}
together with lines for the maps, comparisons and independence assertions.

\paragraph{Checking the exported record in Lean.}  The module \texttt{QSFormal.QamFile} reads such a file and checks it line by line
against the record before it; the executable \texttt{qamcheck} runs this checker on a file.  The checker is a Lean function, and Lean
proves what its success means: every derived claim is fresh, has premises, has at most the status of each of them and shares their frame
(\texttt{derive\_sound}, \texttt{checked\_no\_promotion}); its listed inputs are exactly those of its premises; a conversion uses a
declared map that starts in the premise's frame and ends in the new one (\texttt{convert\_sound}); an independence assertion holds for a
claim without inputs and a comparison only within one frame (\texttt{indep\_sound}, \texttt{compare\_sound}).  The check is independent of
the \TeX{} package: a record edited by hand, for instance with an input deleted from a derived claim, compiles nowhere but is still
caught by \texttt{qamcheck}, and a document compiled with \verb|mode=warning| that contains a promotion produces a record that
\texttt{qamcheck} rejects.  Both cases are part of the continuous integration.  The parser that turns text lines into Lean values is
not verified; only the checker is.

\paragraph{A hash chain from the starting thesis.}  A paper usually starts from a thesis, and every later claim is obtained from it by
further steps, each of which again has a QAM form.  The package records this as a chain of hashes.  \verb|\qamthesis{k}{M}{s}{statement}|
declares a thesis whose statement is part of the record.  Every claim gets a hash, the MD5 sum of its canonical form
\[
  \textit{kind};\ \textit{key};\ \textit{frame};\ \textit{status};\ \textit{inputs};\ \textit{sources};\ \textit{statement},
\]
where the sources are the hashes of its premises.  A derived claim therefore names the exact versions of the claims it was derived from,
and a change anywhere upstream --- one word of the thesis, a status, an input --- changes the hash of every claim below it, while claims
that do not depend on the change keep their hashes.  \verb|\qamhash{k}| prints a short hash, \verb|\qamledger| adds a hash column and the
hash of the whole record, and the export carries hashes, sources and the statement, together with the MD5 sum of the source file.  In the
example above, computed while this paper is compiled, \texttt{gamma} has the hash \texttt{\qamhash{gamma}}, \texttt{counting}
\texttt{\qamhash{counting}}, \texttt{beta} \texttt{\qamhash{beta}} with these two as sources, and \texttt{betaT}
\texttt{\qamhash{betaT}} with \texttt{beta} as source.

The chain is checked twice and independently of \TeX{}: a short script recomputes every hash from the exported fields and the record hash,
and the Lean checker verifies the links --- a derived claim names exactly the recorded hashes of its premises, in order, and no hash occurs
twice (\texttt{link\_derive\_sound}, \texttt{link\_convert\_sound}, \texttt{link\_leaf\_sound}).  Lean does not recompute MD5; a statement
edited by hand without new hashes is caught by the script, a reworded and rehashed thesis whose dependants still name the old hash is caught
by both.  For a release the script writes a manifest with SHA-256 sums of source, PDF and record and the record hash, so that a reader of
the published files can tell which record was checked.  MD5 suffices against accidental change; it does not protect against a deliberate
forgery, for which the manifest's SHA-256 sums are the relevant check.  Equal hashes mean equal records, not true ones.

\paragraph{What the package does not do.}  It checks only what is declared.  A document that states a result without declaring it is
not checked, and a declaration can be wrong: whether \texttt{gamma} really is an input is the author's statement, not the package's.
The package guards against slips in a long record; it does not prove its content.  It is tested by documents that must compile and
documents that must stop with a given error, and the tests run in continuous integration.
