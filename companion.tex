\section{Introduction}
This document is a technical companion to the main Quantum Sphaera manuscript. The main paper remains the leading structural proof and conceptual guide; the present text has the narrower task of making the mathematical backbone more explicit without overwriting the architecture of the source manuscript. In particular, the companion isolates those transitions at which a critical reader would normally ask for sharper definitions, admissibility conditions, operator-level statements, or explicit regime reductions.

The companion follows a strict structure-first principle. It therefore fixes the invariant object and its admissible structural operations before discussing effective equations, projection regimes, or phenomenological readouts. In this logic, the visible sectors are not independent ontological layers but controlled readings of one collapse-centered core. The technical role of the companion is to expose enough of that logic to make the project mathematically inspectable.

The manuscript is organized as follows. Sections~2--5 fix the ground vocabulary, triolic compatibility, the involution--compensator layer, closure, and the first compatibility results. Sections~6--8 derive the equivalence of sector readings, the structural master equation, and the three principal controlled projection regimes. Sections~9--10 connect this structural dynamics to probe geometry, frame-shifted readout, mass, gauge, and Higgs-type coupling data. The discussion and appendices summarize the present mathematical status of the manuscript, collect the principal theorem corridors strengthened in this release, record the current theorem and dependency maps, and indicate the main remaining tasks for later proof expansion.

\section{Structural Preliminaries}

\subsection{Guiding principle}
The main paper fixes the interpretive rule of the framework: the visible sectors are not independent ontological objects, but structurally equivalent readings of a single invariant state. The companion therefore begins with the ground condition and the operators that preserve or reveal it.

\begin{definition}[Collapse state]
The \emph{collapse state} is the invariant structural ground condition
\[
\zminus = 0.
\]
It is the distinguished reference state relative to which the visible Sphaera, triolic decomposition, sector structure, and symmetry readings are interpreted.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Ground carrier]
A \emph{ground carrier} is a structured ambient space together with enough data to formulate the collapse state, a triolic decomposition, an involution, a compensator map, and a closure condition. In operator-theoretic realizations this carrier may be a Hilbert- or Hardy-type space endowed with an anti-unitary involution; in algebraic realizations it may be represented through quaternionic, octonionic, or septenionic liftings.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Triole]
A \emph{triole} is an ordered triple
\[
T=(X_1,X_2,X_\perp)
\]
of structural channels in a common ground carrier such that $X_\perp$ is structurally orthogonal to $X_1$ and $X_2$, while the full triple remains a single admissible reading of the same invariant core.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Structural orthogonality]
Two structural components are \emph{structurally orthogonal} if their admissible coupling vanishes in the relevant closure regime, or survives only as an internally compensated residual term that does not destroy the triolic separation.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Structural sector]
A \emph{structural sector} is an admissible reading of the same ground architecture, for example as collapse reading, triolic reading, compensator-fixed reading, probe reading, or algebraic lifting.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Structural involution]
Let $\mathcal H$ be a ground carrier. A \emph{structural involution} is an anti-unitary map
\[
J:\mathcal H\to\mathcal H,
\qquad J^2 = \mathrm{id},
\]
which encodes the reflected or dual structural reading relevant to the collapse-compatible sector.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Heisenberg compensator]
Given a structural involution $J$, the \emph{Heisenberg compensator} is the map
\[
\HC(f):=\frac12(f+Jf).
\]
It extracts the $J$-compatible component of $f$ and is interpreted as the internal compensating projection toward the closure-compatible sector.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Closure]
A system is \emph{closure-stable} if all admissible states, generated terms, couplings, and readouts remain confined to a single structurally admissible class preserved by the compensator and the allowed evolution maps.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Frame shift]
A \emph{frame shift} is a systematic displacement between the underlying structural coordinates and the coordinates used by a concrete measurement or readout frame, so that observables appear as shifted readings of the same invariant core.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Probe configuration]
A \emph{probe configuration} consists of two probes $P_L,P_R$ that are structurally coordinated with the same ground carrier, together with a third orthogonal component $P_\perp$ acting as a constrained search region. In the standing framework interpretation, each probe may itself carry an internal triolic structure.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Structural equivalence]
Two admissible sector readings are \emph{structurally equivalent} if they are related by a finite chain of admissible carrier maps --- such as triolic re-coordinatizations, involutive reflections, compensator projections, closure-preserving embeddings, or algebraic liftings --- without changing the underlying collapse state.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
In the standing framework interpretation, the Heisenberg compensator is not an auxiliary postulate but emerges from the ground condition and enforces confinement inside the relevant septonionic or meta-septenionic closure sector.
\end{remark}

\section{Triolic Decomposition and Orthogonality}
The triolic decomposition is the first nontrivial structural reading of the ground state. In the main manuscript, the triole is not an added decoration but a coordinate presentation of the same object. In the technical realization, the triolic channels admit algebraic liftings that make orthogonality explicit.

\begin{lemma}[Nontriviality of the triole]
Under the standing assumptions of the framework, the triolic decomposition is not empty and is not reducible to a purely dyadic reading without loss of structural information.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The framework architecture fixes the third component not as a decorative redundancy but as the orthogonal channel required to encode closure, probe separation, and constrained search. Removing the third channel would collapse the distinction between directed transport and orthogonal confinement, and would therefore destroy data that the standing interpretation treats as structurally essential. Hence the triolic decomposition is genuinely nontrivial.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Orthogonal complementability]
Let $(X_1,X_2)$ be an admissible structural pair in a ground carrier. Then there exists a structurally admissible orthogonal complement $X_\perp$, at least up to admissible symmetry equivalence, such that $(X_1,X_2,X_\perp)$ forms a triole.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
In operator-theoretic realizations one passes to the orthogonal complement of the span generated by the admissible pair and then restricts to the closure-compatible component. In algebraic realizations the same role is played by a third distinguished imaginary direction or orthogonal subspace in the corresponding lifting. Since the framework only requires the complement up to admissible structural equivalence, this suffices to produce the desired third channel.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Readout invariance under frame shift]
A frame-shifted readout may alter the observed representation of a structural state, but it does not alter the invariant collapse-centered core read by structurally equivalent sectors.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By definition, the frame shift acts on the readout coordinates rather than on the ground condition itself. The collapse state therefore remains fixed, while the displaced representation changes only the coordinates in which the observer records masses, phases, or couplings. Hence the invariant structural core is preserved.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
This is the precise point at which the project's measurement principle enters: observables may move under readout displacement even though the underlying structure does not.
\end{remark}

\section{Heisenberg Compensator and Closure}
The Heisenberg compensator is the main technical bridge between the structure language of the main stand and the operator-theoretic realization of the Riemann text. In the companion it is the device that turns closure from a verbal constraint into an internal structural condition.

\begin{lemma}[Compensator compatibility]
If a configuration is closure-stable, then the Heisenberg compensator acts structure-internally on that configuration and does not carry admissible states out of the closure-compatible class.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The compensator symmetrizes a state with respect to the structural involution and by construction extracts the component compatible with the admissible closure branch. Since closure-stability means precisely that the admissible class is preserved under the allowed structural maps, the compensator cannot leave that class when acting on closure-stable states.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Closure stability under structurally equivalent readings]
Let $X$ and $Y$ be structurally equivalent sector readings. If $X$ is closure-stable, then $Y$ is closure-stable as well.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Structural equivalence is defined through admissible maps that preserve the collapse state and the admissible carrier structure. Since closure-stability is formulated in terms of preservation under exactly these admissible maps, it is transported from $X$ to $Y$ along any equivalence chain. Therefore closure is invariant under passage between structurally equivalent readings.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Idempotent reading of the compensator]
In a compatible realization in which the involution splits the carrier into compatible and incompatible branches, the Heisenberg compensator acts as the projector onto the compatible branch.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The map $\HC(f)=\frac12(f+Jf)$ is the standard symmetrization with respect to the involution. The result is fixed by $J$ and therefore belongs to the compatible branch; conversely, compatible states are unchanged by the map. In this sense the compensator is idempotent on the compatible reading and acts as the corresponding projector.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Closure projector compatibility]
If $\hat P_{\mathrm{cl}}$ denotes the projector onto the admissible closed sector and $\HC$ is the compensator on the same carrier, then on every closure-stable admissible state one has
\[
\hat P_{\mathrm{cl}}\HC\hat P_{\mathrm{cl}} = \HC\hat P_{\mathrm{cl}}.
\]
In particular, compensator readout of an already closed state remains closed.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $u$ be admissible and closure-stable. Then $\hat P_{\mathrm{cl}}u=u$. By compensator compatibility, $\HC u$ remains inside the admissible class, hence is fixed by $\hat P_{\mathrm{cl}}$. Therefore
\[
\hat P_{\mathrm{cl}}\HC\hat P_{\mathrm{cl}}u=\hat P_{\mathrm{cl}}\HC u = \HC u = \HC\hat P_{\mathrm{cl}}u.
\]
Since this holds on the admissible closed sector, the claimed compatibility follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Defect decomposition of an admissible generator]
Let $T$ be an admissible generator on the ground carrier. Then for every admissible state $u$ one has the decomposition
\[
Tu = \hat P_{\mathrm{cl}}Tu + \mathrm{Res}_{\mathrm{cl}}(T)u,
\]
where the first term is closed and the second term is the unique closure-defect contribution relative to the chosen projector.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Insert the identity $I = \hat P_{\mathrm{cl}} + (I-\hat P_{\mathrm{cl}})$ on the output side of $T$. This gives
\[
Tu = \hat P_{\mathrm{cl}}Tu + (I-\hat P_{\mathrm{cl}})Tu.
\]
For admissible $u$ one has $u=\hat P_{\mathrm{cl}}u$, so the second term is exactly
\[
(I-\hat P_{\mathrm{cl}})T\hat P_{\mathrm{cl}}u = \mathrm{Res}_{\mathrm{cl}}(T)u.
\]
Uniqueness follows from the direct-sum decomposition into closed and complementary parts.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Defect-free admissible evolution]
If all effective generators entering an admissible evolution law are closure-preserving, then the corresponding evolution is defect-free on the closed sector.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply the residue-vanishing lemma to each closure-preserving generator and sum the resulting zero residues. The induced evolution therefore contains no closure-defect contribution on admissible states.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Residue transport through admissible composition]
Let $S$ and $T$ be admissible structural operators. If $S$ preserves the closed sector, then on admissible states one has
\[
\mathrm{Res}_{\mathrm{cl}}(TS)=\mathrm{Res}_{\mathrm{cl}}(T)S.
\]
If both $S$ and $T$ preserve the closed sector, then $TS$ is defect-free.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $u$ be admissible, so $u=\hat P_{\mathrm{cl}}u$. Since $S$ preserves the closed sector, the vector $Su$ is again closed. Hence
\[
\mathrm{Res}_{\mathrm{cl}}(TS)u=(I-\hat P_{\mathrm{cl}})TS\hat P_{\mathrm{cl}}u=(I-\hat P_{\mathrm{cl}})TSu=\mathrm{Res}_{\mathrm{cl}}(T)Su.
\]
This proves the transport identity. If both factors preserve the closed sector, then the right-hand side vanishes by the residue-vanishing lemma, so the composition is defect-free.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Defect cancellation criterion]
Let $T_1$ and $T_2$ be admissible operators on the same ground carrier. If
\[
\mathrm{Res}_{\mathrm{cl}}(T_1)=-\mathrm{Res}_{\mathrm{cl}}(T_2)
\]
on the admissible closed sector, then the sum $T_1+T_2$ is defect-free on that sector.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Residues are linear in the operator. Therefore
\[
\mathrm{Res}_{\mathrm{cl}}(T_1+T_2)=\mathrm{Res}_{\mathrm{cl}}(T_1)+\mathrm{Res}_{\mathrm{cl}}(T_2)=0
\]
on admissible closed states. The summed evolution is therefore defect-free.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Compensated defect cancellation]
If the residue of an admissible generator is cancelled by the residue of a compensator correction term, then the effective generator obtained by adding the correction is defect-free on the closed sector.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply the defect cancellation criterion with $T_2$ chosen as the compensator correction term. When the correction contributes the opposite residue on the admissible sector, the effective sum has vanishing closure residue.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Existence of a closure-compatible ground class]
There exists a nonempty class of states on the ground carrier that is simultaneously triolically readable, orthogonally complementable, compensator-compatible, and closure-stable.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take any admissible realization of the ground carrier carrying a nondegenerate triolic decomposition and a structural involution. By the previous lemmas the triole admits an orthogonal completion and the compensator acts within the compatible branch. Restricting to the compensator-fixed and closure-preserved sector yields a nonempty class with all required properties.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Compensator implies structural binding]
Under the standing assumptions of the framework, the Heisenberg compensator prevents admissible generated terms from leaving the closure-compatible class.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Any admissible term generated from a closure-stable state is first evaluated inside the carrier and then read through the compensator-compatible branch. The previous compatibility lemma shows that the compensator acts internally on the closure class, while closure excludes structurally external leakage. Hence admissible generated terms remain confined to the same class.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
This formalizes the standing framework rule that the compensator prevents structural breakout and confines the dynamics to the admissible septonionic or meta-septenionic layer.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Closure residue]
Let $\hat P_{\mathrm{cl}}$ denote the projector onto the admissible closed sector. For any structural operator $T$ acting on the ground carrier, the associated \emph{closure residue} is
\[
\mathrm{Res}_{\mathrm{cl}}(T):=(I-\hat P_{\mathrm{cl}})T\hat P_{\mathrm{cl}}.
\]
It measures the part of the action of $T$ that attempts to leave the admissible closed sector when evaluated on already admissible states.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Closure defect]
A \emph{closure defect} is any nonzero residue term $\mathrm{Res}_{\mathrm{cl}}(T)$ associated with an admissible structural map, projected derivative, or effective coupling. The admissible regime is defect-free precisely when all structurally allowed generating operators have vanishing closure residue.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Residue vanishes for closure-preserving operators]
If an admissible operator $T$ preserves the closed sector, i.e. $T(\operatorname{Ran} \hat P_{\mathrm{cl}})\subseteq \operatorname{Ran} \hat P_{\mathrm{cl}}$, then $\mathrm{Res}_{\mathrm{cl}}(T)=0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For any $u$ in the carrier, the vector $\hat P_{\mathrm{cl}}u$ already lies in the closed sector. By the assumed preservation property, $T\hat P_{\mathrm{cl}}u$ again lies in the closed sector and is therefore fixed by $\hat P_{\mathrm{cl}}$. Applying $(I-\hat P_{\mathrm{cl}})$ to such a vector yields zero. Hence the residue vanishes identically.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Compensator suppression of closure defects]
Within the admissible companion regime, every compensator-stable closure-preserving generator is defect-free. More generally, any admissible generated term may be decomposed into a closed contribution and a closure residue, and the residue vanishes once the compensator and closure conditions are simultaneously imposed.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The decomposition is obtained by inserting $I=\hat P_{\mathrm{cl}}+(I-\hat P_{\mathrm{cl}})$ on the output side of an admissible generator. The first summand is the closed contribution and the second is exactly the closure residue. If the generator is closure-preserving, the previous lemma gives vanishing residue. If, in addition, the generator is read through the compensator-compatible branch, then any remaining incompatible component is removed by the compensator projector, so no admissible defect survives in the effective closed dynamics.
\end{proof}




\begin{lemma}[Closure stability under admissible deformation]
Let $\{T_\varepsilon\}_{|\varepsilon|<\varepsilon_0}$ be an admissible deformation family of structural operators with $T_0$ closure-preserving and with deformation residue $\mathrm{Res}_{\mathrm{cl}}(T_\varepsilon-T_0)$ compensated by the same Heisenberg branch. Then for sufficiently small admissible $\varepsilon$, the deformed operator remains closure-compatible on the closed sector.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By defect decomposition, the only obstruction to closure-compatibility is the complementary residue term. The hypothesis states that the deformation residue remains inside the same compensator-controlled branch, so the deformed operator differs from the closure-preserving one only by an internally compensated admissible correction. For sufficiently small admissible deformation parameter this correction cannot open a new external leakage channel, hence closure-compatibility is preserved.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Compatibility persists under closure-admissible deformation]
The compatible structural class identified in the previous existence proposition remains nonempty and admissible under every sufficiently small closure-admissible deformation of its effective generators.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Start from one compatible representative provided by the existence proposition. Apply the previous deformation lemma to each admissible generator entering the corresponding realization. Since closure, compensator branch, and orthogonal triolic structure are preserved under the allowed small deformation, the deformed realization remains inside the same compatible class. Nonemptiness therefore persists.
\end{proof}

\section{Structural Existence and Compatibility}
The first technical task of the companion is to show that the tuple consisting of collapse state, triolic decomposition, involution, compensator, and closure condition is not only nonempty but mutually compatible at the structural level.

\begin{proposition}[Minimal compatibility scaffold]
Suppose a ground carrier admits:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item a nontrivial triolic decomposition,
    \item a structural involution $J$,
    \item a compensator $\HC$ induced by $J$, and
    \item a closure sector preserved by the admissible structural maps.
\end{enumerate}
Then the structural system is well-posed at the abstract level and supports a nontrivial collapse-compatible sector.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Nontriviality follows from the nondegenerate triolic decomposition. The involution and the associated compensator select a compatible branch, and closure preserves that branch under admissible structural operations. Hence the tuple defines a coherent structural system with a distinguished collapse-compatible sector.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Structural compatibility theorem]
The collapse state, triolic decomposition, structural orthogonality, Heisenberg compensator, and closure condition are jointly compatible within every admissible realization of the companion framework.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The collapse state fixes the invariant core. By the triolic lemmas there is a nontrivial three-channel reading with an admissible orthogonal complement. By the compensator lemmas, the involution-induced compensator acts internally on closure-stable states and projects to the compatible branch. By the existence proposition there is a nonempty class on which all of these structures are simultaneously realized. Therefore the listed ingredients are jointly compatible.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Stability of admissible observables]
Every observable obtained by composing a closure-preserving operator with compensator readout remains an observable of the same admissible structural class.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Closure-preserving operators keep admissible states inside the closed sector, and the closure-projector compatibility lemma shows that compensator readout of such states remains closed. Hence the composed observable never leaves the admissible class.
\end{proof}

\section{Structural Equivalence and Sector Relations}
Different visible sectors are interpreted as distinct readings of the same invariant state rather than unrelated objects. The companion's role is to formulate this principle in a technically sharper form.

\begin{proposition}[Equivalence of visible sector readings]
Assume the ground carrier admits a collapse state $\zminus=0$, a nondegenerate triolic decomposition, a structural involution $J$, and a compensator-stable closure sector. Then the following are structurally equivalent readings of a single invariant object:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item the collapse-compatible sector itself,
    \item its triolic coordinate presentations,
    \item the compensator-fixed realization $\HC(f)$ of admissible states, and
    \item algebraic liftings into quaternionic, octonionic, or septonian realizations that preserve triolic orthogonality and closure.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The collapse state is fixed by assumption. A nondegenerate triolic decomposition gives coordinate readings of the same object rather than additional objects. The involution and compensator preserve the collapse-compatible sector and therefore provide admissible re-readings inside the same closure class. Finally, algebraic liftings are admissible precisely because they preserve the orthogonality and confinement data required by the carrier. Hence all displayed realizations represent the same invariant object in different structural languages.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Probe-sector relation]
Let $\Pi_L$ and $\Pi_R$ be orthogonal probe projectors and let $P_\perp$ project onto the orthogonal complement. Then the triple
\[
(\mathrm{ran}(\Pi_L),\mathrm{ran}(\Pi_R),\mathrm{ran}(P_\perp))
\]
forms a triolic decomposition at the probe level, with the third sector acting as a constrained search region.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Orthogonality of the probe projectors gives separation of the left and right channels. By construction, the orthogonal complement is perpendicular to both. Hence the probe configuration inherits the same triolic logic as the base carrier: two directed channels together with a third orthogonal search sector.
\end{proof}


\begin{lemma}[Equivalence preserves orthogonality data]
If two sector readings are structurally equivalent, then the orthogonality relations among their triolic channels are preserved up to admissible symmetry equivalence.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Structural equivalence is generated by admissible maps that preserve the collapse state, the triolic architecture, and closure. Such maps may re-coordinate the channels, but they cannot remove the third orthogonal role without leaving the admissible class. Therefore orthogonality data are preserved up to the allowed symmetries of the carrier.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Equivalence preserves compensator-fixed content]
Let $X$ and $Y$ be structurally equivalent readings. Then compensator-fixed data in $X$ correspond to compensator-fixed data in $Y$ under the admissible equivalence chain.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Each admissible map in the equivalence chain preserves the closure-compatible branch and therefore transports the compensator-fixed component of an admissible state to the compensator-fixed component of its image. Composing these maps yields the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Structural equivalence theorem]
Collapse reading, triolic reading, compensator-fixed reading, and admissible algebraic liftings are structurally equivalent representations of the same collapse-centered invariant core.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The previous equivalence proposition identifies the admissible readings and proves that each preserves the same collapse state. Closure stability under structurally equivalent readings guarantees that passage between them does not destroy admissibility, and readout invariance under frame shift shows that observational displacement does not alter the invariant core. Hence all listed readings are structurally equivalent representations of the same object.
\end{proof}


\begin{lemma}[Equivalence preserves active closure profile]
Let $X$ and $Y$ be structurally equivalent admissible readings. Then the active closure profile of every admissible state in $X$ is transported to the same closure profile in $Y$ up to admissible internal redistribution.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Structural equivalence is generated by admissible maps that preserve the collapse state, the closure-compatible branch, and the compensator-fixed content. Therefore the decomposition of an admissible state into active closed contribution and internally redistributed residue is preserved under passage from $X$ to $Y$. Since admissible redistribution is internal by definition, the active closure profile is unchanged as a structural datum.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Projection-class invariance of the collapse kernel]
All structurally equivalent admissible readings determine the same collapse-centered invariant kernel at the level of projection classes.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The structural equivalence theorem identifies collapse, triolic, compensator-fixed, and admissible lifted readings as presentations of one invariant object. The previous lemma shows that their active closure profile is preserved up to internal redistribution. Hence no admissible passage between structurally equivalent readings can generate a distinct collapse kernel; it can only change the representative inside the same projection class.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Derived tools from the structural equivalence corridor.}
The equivalence block now yields a second family of reusable tools:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2.2em]
    \item \textbf{Orthogonality-preservation test:} verify that an admissible sector change preserves the triolic orthogonality data up to allowed symmetries.
    \item \textbf{Compensator-fixed transfer rule:} transport compensator-fixed content between equivalent readings without recomputing the full admissibility chain.
    \item \textbf{Closure-profile transport shortcut:} compare active closure content across equivalent sectors up to internal redistribution.

This is the companion version of the project's standing rule that Sphaera, triole, sector decomposition, and compensator output are not separate ontological layers. They are distinct mathematically useful readings of the same collapse architecture.
\end{remark}
\section{Derivation of the Structural Master Equation}
The main paper already suggests that the effective dynamics should be first-order, closure-preserving, and compatible with both the probe sector and the compensator sector. The companion now sharpens this into a short derivation chain: first the admissible derivative is fixed, then the resonance content is constrained, and finally the four structural slots of the master equation are identified.

\begin{definition}[Closure-compatible covariant derivative]
Let $\hat P_{\mathrm{cl}}$ denote the projector onto the admissible closure sector, let $\mathcal A_\mu$ be an effective connection, let $n_\mu$ denote the distinguished structural axis, and let $\mathcal H_C$ denote the compensator field. The \emph{closure-compatible covariant derivative} is
\[
\mathcal D_\mu
:=
\partial_\mu
+ i\mathcal A_\mu
+ \kappa\,\HC\,n_\mu
+ \eta\,(\mathbf 1-\hat P_{\mathrm{cl}})\,n_\mu,
\]
where $\kappa,\eta\in\mathbb R$ are effective couplings.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
The third term stabilizes the compensator-compatible branch, while the fourth penalizes drift away from the admissible closure sector. This matches the standing project reading that closure is not decorative but dynamically enforced.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Necessity of the closure slot]
Any first-order structural dynamics that is intended to preserve admissibility must contain a term that distinguishes the closure-compatible branch from its complement.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Without such a term, the dynamics cannot detect whether an evolved state remains in the admissible closure sector or drifts into its complement. But closure in the companion is not merely interpretive; it is part of the structural definition of admissibility.
